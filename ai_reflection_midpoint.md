Throughout the project, we used Generative AI as a support tool, mainly for exploring analysis options and improving our figures. It came up with a few ideas we probably wouldn’t have tried otherwise. But there also were some contradictional suggestions that ideally should’ve been reviewed or refined in a more serious way. 

The AI assistance was very helpful with spotting the possible problems. For example, it pointed out that differences between age groups might partly come from differences between individual donors rather than from the age itself, and suggested to add donor identities to the visuals for the general UMAP. However, we still wanted to treat donor information as some context for better biological interpretations, rather than as some separate plot that must be in our report. We still keep in mind that, since each group contains a different set of donors, obvious differences between the age groups cannot be attributed only to the age. Of course, the real scientific work with a similar or even more complex task requires testing and diminishing the batch-effect. 

Another direction where AI helped the team by the assistance is gene filtering/masking.  Even with the fact that it was mostly the human-pair part, we should give some credit to the agent’s technical help. We managed to understand how the dataset can be processed. 

At some points AI decisions didn’t seem quite reliable. Discussing different approaches to plot the UMAPs, AI did not always want to make it interactive, even though for us it was obvious that with such large and complex dataset the visuals should be interactive. Or, in case of gene filtering, AI proposed the different way to look at the data for masking out genes, but in fact the proposed approach did not quite made sense. 

For us, AI was most helpful for brainstorming some modifications and expanding the code implementation. We still had to check whether the methods made sense scientifically, test the code ourselves, and make the final decisions. In the end, we saw it more as an assistant that helps you see more options than as something that can judge which analysis is the right one.
